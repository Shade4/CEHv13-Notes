# 04 — Footprinting Through Social Networking Sites

Social networks can expose organizational and human information.

## Information that may be publicly visible

- Employee names
- Job titles
- Departments
- Projects
- Locations
- Conferences
- Technology discussions
- Hiring information
- Organizational changes
- Vendor relationships

## Why employee roles matter

A security team can learn about an organization's structure from public professional profiles.

For example:

```text
Employee
 └── Job title
      └── Department
           └── Technology/project
                └── Possible organizational dependency
```

This can help an authorized assessor understand the organization's attack surface.

## Social-media analysis

Look for:

- Repeated company naming conventions
- Technology keywords
- Project names
- Office locations
- Public contact channels
- Publicly discussed incidents
- Security awareness patterns

## Geolocation and oversharing

Photos and posts may unintentionally reveal:

- Office layouts
- Badges
- Screens
- Whiteboards
- Travel plans
- Locations
- Meeting schedules

Defensive lesson:

> Public social information should be treated as part of the organization's external attack surface.

## Ethical boundary

Do not create fake identities to manipulate people unless the engagement explicitly authorizes a controlled social-engineering test and defines the rules.

Do not collect unnecessary personal information.

## Defensive controls

- Security-awareness training
- Social-media policy
- Minimize unnecessary sensitive disclosures
- Disable unnecessary geolocation features
- Review photos before publication
- Protect employee directories
- Deactivate former employee accounts
- Train staff to identify social-engineering attempts
